# 0.0.23 plan: rendering performance, Noctiluca's images, Doom's core gaps and `rich-rst` 2.2.0

**Status:** started on 2026-10-25 as a list of what [0.0.22](0.0.22.md)
handed on. It is filled in once 0.0.22's cohort is tagged; 0.0.22 was
published the same day, and the versions are open (see
[Package impact](#package-impact)). It belongs to
stage 2 of the [plan to 1.0.0](1.0.0.md).

## Handed on from 0.0.22

Both were the maintainer's call on 2026-10-25.

### 1. rs-rich-doom's core gaps: art, intuituive, record

rs-rich-doom, rebuilt on the 0.0.21 crates, lists what it still builds for
itself in [its core gaps](../design/doom-core-gaps.md). Each is generic:
each names a use other than Doom, and nothing of Doom's (WAD, DMX, MUS,
OPL, its keys) goes into core. Gap 4, made-up releases with a first-press
hold and a repeat hold, lands in 0.0.22.

- **Pictures:** a live frame image node in intuiTUIve's `art` plugin
  (gap 1); a public Sixel encoder for indices and a palette (gap 2) and
  Kitty raw-pixel transmission (gap 3) in rs-rich-art.
- **The clock and stats:** a worker that steps in lockstep with the app's
  clock (gap 5) and draw timings in `FrameStats` (gap 6).
- **Audio:** a voice mixer for effects (gap 7), a render-callback output
  for synthesizers (gap 8), a paced test sink and capture (gap 9), and
  ALSA's messages kept off the screen for the stream's life (gap 10).
- **Pixels:** a palette-keeping two-colour rule for Braille and quadrants
  (gap 11) and an aspect-fitted pixel buffer (gap 12).
- **Recordings:** sound in `rich record` tapes (gap 13).

Each gap's entry has its proposed API and acceptance tests. When one
lands, rs-rich-doom drops its own version of it.

**Status (2026-10-26, branch `023-doom`):** gaps 1, 3 (finished here with
`transmit_raw`, `transmit_rgb`, placement ids and zlib) and 6 were closed
by waves 1 and 2, and gap 4 by 0.0.22. Built here: gap 2
(`sixel::encode_palette`, `index_exact`), 11 (`Node::pixel_colors`), 12
(`PixelBuf::draw_fitted`), 5 (`stepped_worker`), 9
(`OutputOptions::null`, `Output::tap`), 7 (`audio::Mixer`), 8
(`audio::output_with`) and 10 (ALSA's handler on each stream's thread,
thread-local rather than process-wide). Gap 13, sound in tapes, waits
on the maintainer's choice of how the sound reaches the recorder; the
gaps doc lists the options. Each gap's entry says where it landed.

### 4. Noctiluca's media, to the PoC's look: noctiluca, embed

The maintainer's calls on 2026-10-25: images in 0.0.23, video in 0.0.24.
Later that day, the maintainer asked for Noctiluca to look like their PoC
in 0.0.23, so video and the rest of 0.0.24 moved here. This is phases 0.3
(apart from its layout fixtures) and 0.4 of the
[design note](../design/browser.md).

**The target is the PoC's evidence** (`archived-bbc-news.png`,
`archived-youtube-watch.png`):

- page text as terminal text, with real pictures inline where the page
  puts them, each with its caption;
- a video's poster with a play mark, and the video playing in its region
  once started;
- a header with the mode and the URL, and a status line with the media
  state (`media PLAYING`), the row, and the keys.

A news-page fixture (local, no network) is recorded by a tape, and its
screenshot sits beside the PoC's in the guide.

**Images:**

- **Pictures in the projection view:** each media region shows the image
  itself, not the alt text and a shaded box. Kitty, Sixel and iTerm2 draw
  real pixels; other terminals get half blocks.
- **Pictures partly in view:** a Sixel or iTerm2 picture is drawn clipped
  to the rows shown, not as half blocks until all of it is on screen.
  Kitty already works row by row.
- **Animated GIFs:** they play in their region within the frame budget.
  Kitty's own animation frames are used where the terminal has them, and
  freezing a GIF stops its bytes.
- **Lazy images load:** with Chrome's scroll following ours (the
  [rendering plan](../design/render-performance.md)), `loading=lazy`
  images below the fold load as they come into view.
- **The recordings show them:** the replayed snapshots behind the tapes
  carry image stills, so the docs' recordings show pictures.

It builds on the rendering plan's image layer: a picture is uploaded once
and placed by reference, and a live frame node replaces Doom's gap 1.

**Video:**

- **A playing video plays in its region.** Chrome's screencast is cropped
  to that region, only while the video plays.
- **Controls:** play, pause, seek and mute, both from the media controls
  and from keys. Sound already plays with `--audio` since 0.0.22.
- **A frame budget per region.** Each region's frame rate comes from a
  total byte budget. The PoC's 8 fps is a ceiling, not a target.
- **Stepping down on slow terminals:** Kitty, then iTerm2, then Sixel,
  then quadrant or half-block art. Over SSH, video starts as character
  art.
- **Tests:**
  - the PoC's `motion.webm` fixture plays, pauses and seeks;
  - the region's frame rate stays under its cap, counted in bytes over two
    seconds;
  - over a slow simulated terminal, it steps down to character art;
  - the about page's codec table matches `canPlayType`.

  Done on branch `023-video` (pushed to
  `claude/next-priority-items-3c5p8e-wip-video`), on A's picture API:
  all four tests pass against Chrome for Testing as a normal user, with
  the header and status bar of the PoC. The budget is a guess until the
  painter measures the terminal's output rate (see the design note's
  [Media regions](../design/browser.md#media-regions)).

**The maintainer's direction, 2026-10-25, later that day:**

- **Rendered by intuiTUIve and Noctiluca.** Every cell and every pixel
  comes from our own stack: Noctiluca's model and projection, drawn by
  intuiTUIve's nodes and painter, pictures encoded by rs-rich-art and
  intuiTUIve's `art` feature (Kitty, Sixel, iTerm2, half blocks). No
  chafa or other converter, and no replay of a captured page standing in
  for rendering. Chrome supplies only the DOM, the layout, resource bytes
  and, while a video plays, cropped screencast frames. The PoC's
  "CHAFA-SIXEL" is the look to match, not the means.
- **No site-specific code.** Nothing checks for a site: no host names, no
  per-site selectors, no "this is a chat" case. Pictures come from `<img>`,
  `<picture>` and CSS background images; video and audio from `<video>`
  and `<audio>`, with their poster, controls and play state; side by side
  from grids and flex rows, by geometry; live updates from DOM mutations
  plus ARIA `role=log` and `aria-live`, so any live log sticks to the
  bottom; inputs and buttons from their roles and names. Fixtures are
  test pages named by what they exercise, in ordinary HTML. A test runs
  the same projection on two differently built pages with the same
  constructs (a log as a `<ul aria-live>` and as a `<div role=log>`) and
  gets the same result.
- **Terminal adaptations are welcome when generic:** each is a named,
  documented rule about constructs and cells, applied to every page alike,
  with a test on a fixture. They are listed in the design note's
  [Terminal adaptations](../design/browser.md#terminal-adaptations).
- **A video page a bit more visual than the PoC's:** a local fixture
  (`media-grid.html`, no network, our own pictures) with a player whose
  poster then `motion.webm` plays in place, a row of thumbnails with
  titles under them side by side, small avatars, and a compact comment
  list under the player. Its own tape and a screenshot in the guide.
- **A live stream page with chat:** a local fixture (`stream-with-log.html`)
  with the video region playing `motion.webm` in a loop as the stream, the
  channel's name, avatar and title, and a `role=log` panel beside it that
  the page's own script fills on a timer. Noctiluca follows the log
  through the change ping without reading the whole page again, sticks
  to its bottom unless the user scrolled up, shows each message's
  coloured name and its small pictures (badges, emotes), and its input
  types and sends (the page echoes the message). Its own tape and a
  screenshot in the guide. Tests: new messages appear within the budget
  without a full re-read; stick-to-bottom holds and scrolling up pauses
  it; a sent message reads back; the video keeps playing while the log
  updates, within its byte cap.

**Where the picture work goes (the maintainer's call, 2026-10-25):**
picture handling is rs-rich-art's, with no new crate. Its `picture`
feature holds a picture's identity and version, decoding, fitting, every
protocol's encoding, cells for composing into rows, and byte accounting.
Its `frames` feature, next to `gif` and with no new dependencies, is
video frame display: RGBA frames to Kitty, Sixel, iTerm2 and blocks
(Kitty's raw `f=32`, Doom's gap 3), pacing within a byte budget, and the
step down. Decoding video files is out of scope (a separate
`rs-rich-video` crate later, if ever), and Chrome's screencast stays in
rs-rich-embed. intuiTUIve's `art` feature holds only the glue: the live
frame node (`picture_of`, Doom's gap 1 in part) and the placement.
Noctiluca decides what shows where. The API is sketched in the design
note's [Media regions](../design/browser.md#media-regions).

### 5. Rendering performance: ext, intuituive, noctiluca, web

The [rendering plan](../design/render-performance.md):

- **Measuring first:** frame stats, a timedemo and a benchmark.
- **Then optimising** what the profile shows.
- **Paint strategies, with `best`:** the default from this release,
  since 0.0.24 folded in. It becomes the default only where the
  benchmark shows it at least matching `cells` on every scene; a scene
  that loses gets its heuristic fixed first, or `best` ships opt-in.
- **A virtual scroll view** with a near-end hook.
- **Infinite scroll in Noctiluca.**
- **rs-rich-web on the damage list.**

Work started on 2026-10-25 on two branches. Frame stats, the timedemo,
the paint benchmark and the strategies landed on `023-core`, with `best`
still opt-in; the virtual scroll view, Noctiluca's infinite scroll and
the web's damage list on `023-scroll`.

### 7. Lighter strings in core, and rs-rich-ext's fast path: rich, ext, intuituive

- **The decision:** the maintainer's call on 2026-10-25, "make the strings
  lighter", brought forward from the 0.1.0 API review.
- **The change:** rich's per-cell public strings (`Segment.text`,
  `Color.name`) move to a light string type, so a short run or a hex colour
  never allocates.
- **The guarantees:** output stays byte-identical, proven by the goldens,
  the parity tables and the fuzzer. Callers keep working through deref
  and `as_str`.
- **Breaking for rs-rich 0.0.x,** with a migration note in the release
  notes.
- **Measured:** against rs-rich-ext's fast path, which stays the default
  for ext and intuiTUIve.

**Landed** on the `023-core` branch, with rs-rich-ext's fast path (also
the maintainer's call on 2026-10-25). The measurements are in [the
render performance note](../design/render-performance.md#the-two-paths-side-by-side).

- **rs-rich (breaking):** `Segment.text` and `Color.name` are
  `rich::CompactString`s. Output is byte-identical.
- **rs-rich-ext:** the fast path (`surface::Draw`, `TextRun`,
  `Screen::write_runs`), implemented by the charts.
- **intuiTUIve:** `Canvas::draw`, `drawn`, `drawn_described`; the `viz`
  nodes draw through the fast path.

**The maintainer's decisions of 2026-10-25** (the core agent's three
questions, all accepted):

1. **The `CompactString` break goes past the two fields.** `Segment::new`
   no longer takes a `char`, and a value that is `impl Into<String>` must
   become a `String` first. The migration note says both.
2. **compact_str 0.9 is a new third-party dependency of `rs-rich`**, the
   mirror crate. The mirror accepts it for byte-identical speed;
   docs/PORTING.md's "Performance notes" records it.
3. **intuiTUIve's `described` stays on rich's path.** `drawn_described` is
   the fast alternative, so third-party `AccessibleText` types keep
   working.

**For the release notes, under Migration:**

> `Segment.text` and `Color.name` are now `rich::CompactString`
> (`compact_str`'s, kept inline up to 24 bytes) instead of `String`.
> Reading them is unchanged: they deref to `&str` and compare with `&str`
> and `String`. Where your code moved or cloned one into a `String`, write
> `.to_string()` (or `.into_string()` to move it; `.as_str()` to borrow).
> Where it assigned a `String` to one, add `.into()`. `Segment::new` and
> `Segment::control` take `impl Into<CompactString>`: `&str`, `String`,
> `&String`, `Cow<str>` and `Box<str>` work as before; a `char` or an
> `impl Into<String>` needs converting to a `String` first. The Python
> package is unchanged.
